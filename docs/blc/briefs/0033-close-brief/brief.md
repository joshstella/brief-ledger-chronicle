# The close nothing writes

**Serial:** #0033 · **Created:** 2026-10-08T13:42:58Z · **Author:** josh.stella@gmail.com · **Depends on:** —

Three places name `brief/<serial>-closeout`: `blc-next-brief-phase` step 8 and
`docs/blc/briefs/README.md` twice. All three say the branch carries the close. None says
what the close writes. No skill performs it.

The survey of all 32 closed ledgers:

- 16 have no `Closed:` date. Not old decay — #0023 to #0028 are six consecutive recent
  closes without it, while #0007, #0011 and #0013 have it.
- The closing section has been written as `Closeout`, `Closeout, <date>`, `Close — <date>`,
  `Open after close`, `Big decisions`, `Complications`, or omitted.
- A stable shape — `What shipped`, `What the record shows that the brief did not predict`,
  `Open after close` — appears only in #0029 to #0032. Those are the last four and they
  agree because one agent held the convention in context across one session. The
  convention is in nobody's file.

This is the shape #0032 was filed against: a step that a document describes and no writer
performs. There it was the draft. Here it is the close.

Settled before filing:

1. A clause and a skill, clause first. The clause states what a close must contain, then
   the skill writes something with a specification to satisfy instead of inventing one.
2. The 16 ledgers that would fail the clause are reported, not gated. They are record, and
   this project does not rewrite record to make a later rule true.

What a skill cannot do is the reason the clause comes first. The judgment half of a close —
what shipped, what the brief failed to predict, what stays open — is prose an agent writes
and nothing checks. The mechanical half is the status line, the `Closed:` date, the phase
row, the declaration cleanup, and the test-exempt PR. The mechanical half is the half that
is measurably inconsistent, and it is the half a clause can hold.
